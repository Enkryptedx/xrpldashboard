## 1. CLARITY Act — cloture vote queued for the week of Sept 15

The US Senate returns from recess on 2026-09-14 with cloture on the motion to proceed to H.R. 3633 — the *Digital Asset Market Clarity Act of 2025* — waiting for it. Majority Leader John Thune filed the motion on 2026-08-08 before the August recess; the invocation vote is [scheduled for the week of Sept 15](https://xrpldashboard.com/regulation) and needs 60 senators to succeed. This is a procedural gate, not final passage — a `yes` opens Senate floor debate on the bill; a `no` stalls it there. The bill passed the House 294–134 on 2025-07-17 (Roll Call #199) and was reported out of Senate Banking 15–9 on 2026-05-14. The full timeline, primary-source links, and the "what has NOT happened" list live at [/regulation](https://xrpldashboard.com/regulation) (last verified 2026-09-06). ## 2. XRPL Token Registry launched — 203 ticker collisions in the curator queue

The XRPL Token Registry shipped this week with taxonomy v1.0.0: 12 real categories plus 2 review-status values, all documented at [/registry/taxonomy](https://xrpldashboard.com/registry/taxonomy). The 2026-09-08 signed registry snapshot records **16,742 token_facts rows** and **385 category-current rows** ([source: /.well-known/registry/2026-09-08.json](https://xrpldashboard.com/.well-known/registry/2026-09-08.json)). Of those, **203 rows carry the `ticker_collision` flag** — tokens whose decoded currency code matches a well-known off-chain symbol (USDT, USDC, BTC, ETH, RLUSD, LTC, …) while the issuer address is not on the canonical whitelist. That count dropped from 205 to 203 on 2026-09-08 when the Midas / Axelar bridge issuer whitelist landed. `ticker_collision` is a mechanical statement about issuer provenance, not a verdict on the token or its issuer — definitions and boundaries at [/registry/taxonomy#ticker_collision](https://xrpldashboard.com/registry/taxonomy). ## 3. xrpl_stream data-loss fix + honest undercount disclosure

On 2026-09-07 18:56 UTC we raised the `send_queue_limit` on the local rippled node's subscription stream from the default (100) to 500. The default was quietly kicking the loopback walker off with a "client is too slow" disconnect whenever inbound activity outran the reader — a silent data-loss mode under load, not a hard failure that would page. Post-fix reconciliation against an independent trace puts the historical undercount at **roughly 35%, within a plausible 12–43% band** depending on the sampled window. That range is now disclosed on [/methodology](https://xrpldashboard.com/methodology). Separately, and worth admitting: [/methodology](https://xrpldashboard.com/methodology) itself was offline for approximately 56 hours earlier in the week before this fix landed — an outage of the very page that documents our limitations. It is on the record here rather than smoothed over. ## 5. Signed receipts live on /check.json

On **2026-09-08 02:33 UTC** every response from `/check.json` began carrying a verifiable Ed25519 signature. The signing key is the receipt/registry keypair whose fingerprint is `A4:0F:B1:0A:9D:33:64:03` — the same key that signed the [2026-09-08 registry snapshot](https://xrpldashboard.com/.well-known/registry/2026-09-08.json) at 06:15 UTC. Signatures use the `xrpldashboard/receipt/v1` domain separator, prepended before Ed25519 verify so a receipt signature can never be replayed as a registry-envelope signature (or vice-versa). The public key is published at [/.well-known/snapshots/receipt_pubkey.pem](https://xrpldashboard.com/.well-known/snapshots/receipt_pubkey.pem). A live example any reader can inspect: [`/check.json?q=rL2yMECEyUT94pLDrAcetMNMG1H4xqpNWQ`](https://xrpldashboard.com/check.json?q=rL2yMECEyUT94pLDrAcetMNMG1H4xqpNWQ) (the on-XRPL Anchor account itself). One fingerprint now covers two attestation surfaces: per-request receipts and the daily registry snapshot.
