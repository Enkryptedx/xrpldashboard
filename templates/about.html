About xrpldashboard

A public-good ledger dashboard for the XRP Ledger. Free for retail users, forever. A public-utility approach to ledger data: opinionated about clarity, no token, no DEX, no upsells.

Mission

Help XRP holders understand on-chain activity. Not get rich. Not sell liquidity. Not push tokens. Just data, explained well, for people who want to understand what they own.

Principles

  1. Retail-free, forever. No paywall will ever bump a retail user.
  2. Plain language over jargon. Every metric is explained simply.
  3. Link out generously. We're a dashboard, not a directory — we send you to XRPSCAN, Bithomp, and others for the granular details they do better.
  4. Editorial voice is part of the product. We make choices about what to show and how — that’s the work.
  5. Trust over features. We never sensationalize. We never imply intent we can’t verify. We’d rather omit than overstate.
  6. Mission-first. The funding model serves the mission, not the other way around.
  7. Open source. MIT-licensed. The full source is on GitHub — anyone can fork, audit, or self-host.
  8. Transparent metrics. Our visitor analytics and our infrastructure status (/health) are both publicly viewable. Nothing to hide.

How it works

Every live number on this site is computed directly from the XRP Ledger. We do not aggregate or re-publish data from third-party APIs (Bithomp, XRPSCAN, xrpl.to, XPMarket, DefiLlama). Their products are good — but if we depended on them for our data, we’d just be a wrapper for their work.

Live ledger data

Comes from public XRPL nodes (currently Ripple’s s1.ripple.com and s2.ripple.com). A node is just a peer in the consensus network — it can’t lie about ledger contents without the rest of the network rejecting it. The data is canonical. As traffic grows, the plan is to run our own rippled node so we don’t depend on anyone else’s availability.

Reference labels

Token display names and named-account labels are hand-curated in our open-source repo. Each label has a verifiable source URL. Contributions go through public PR review (see TOKEN_NAMES.md and KNOWN_ACCOUNTS.md). Same pattern mempool.space uses for its mining-pool list.

Cross-checking

We link out to XRPSCAN, Bithomp, and other sources so you can verify our numbers against alternative readings whenever you want. Linking out for verification strengthens the trust principle, not weakens it.

Methodology notes

  • Whale movement — any single XRP transaction of 50,000 XRP or more. Smaller transfers don’t appear on /whales.
  • Token labels — only displayed when we can cite a first-party source for the issuer (exchange page, project .toml, etc.). Unverified issuers render unlabeled rather than guessed.

If we ever start depending on a third-party live-data source, that dependency will be disclosed on this page.

How it’s funded

Hosting and infrastructure currently run a few dollars a month, covered out of pocket. As the project grows we’ll be applying for XRPL ecosystem grants, and donations are welcome (see contact email). Eventually a paid institutional tier (API access, alerts, custom dashboards) will help fund the public retail dashboard, which stays free forever.

Who’s behind it

Charlie Bruce is the developer behind xrpldashboard. He built it to bring utility and understanding to everyone who interacts with the XRP Ledger — retail holders and institutional investors alike. The goal isn’t to serve any particular issuer or exchange; it’s to make the ledger legible to the people who use it. Feedback, corrections, and contributions are welcome — see Get involved below.
